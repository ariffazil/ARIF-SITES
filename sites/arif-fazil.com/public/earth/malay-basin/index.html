01How the basin got here

The Malay Basin is elongate — NW–SE, parallel to the Peninsular Malaysia coastline. It began as a back-arc rift in the Late Eocene, sagged through the Oligocene–Early Miocene, then inverted in the Middle–Late Miocene when regional compression squeezed the older normal faults into anticlinal traps. The post-inversion Pliocene–Recent phase is a quiet regional subsidence — but the basin is still structurally alive.

L. EOCENE — OLIGOCENE · SYNRIFT

Group L/M · the basin is born

Extensional rift basin formation. Deposition of continental, lacustrine sediment in half-grabens. Source rocks for the entire petroleum system are laid down here. The crust stretches. The basement drops. The lake forms. Dead plankton begin their slow conversion to oil.

EARLY MIOCENE · TRANSITION

Group K, J, I · sag phase

The rift cools. The basin sags thermally. Fluvial-deltaic systems prograde from the uplifted margins — Group K coarse sandstones at the base, Group J shales, Group I siltstones and coastal-plain sands. This is the first major reservoir package.

MIDDLE–LATE MIOCENE · INVERSION

Group H, F, E, D · the squeeze

Regional compression inverts the older normal faults. The faults reverse, pushing the hanging wall up. Anticlinal closures form. The Middle Miocene Anticline Play — the proven, highly successful play of the Malay Basin — is born here. Most of the discovered gas and oil is trapped in these Miocene anticlines.

PLIOCENE — PRESENT · POST-INVERSION

Group A, B, C · the quiet phase

Regional subsidence. Marine transgression. The basin fills with fine-grained marine sediments. The deformation has stopped, but the basin is still settling and still leaking at the edges. The Western Hinge Fault zone — a NE–SW trending basement fault — is the active focus of the 2024–2025 wildcat campaign.

The four-act structure is the same shape as the Kinabalu Basin, but the timing is different. Where Kinabalu deformed by collision and obduction, Malay deformed by intra-cratonic inversion — a continent compressing its own back-arc. The result is the same: hydrocarbons trapped in fold-thrust structures, waiting for a drill bit.

02What's in the section

Eight named groups, ~6–12 km of total section, hydrocarbon-bearing from top to bottom. The Malay Basin uses a letter scheme (Group A through M) rather than named formations — a PETRONAS convention that organises the section by major transgressive cycles.

For the curious — why the letter scheme?

The Malay Basin predates the global formation nomenclature. PETRONAS geologists in the 1970s–80s mapped the section from offshore wells and seismic, and assigned letter labels (A through M) from top to bottom, each letter representing a major transgressive cycle. The labels stuck. The advantage: every geoscientist working the basin uses the same scheme, and the letter cycles are visible in well logs as fining-upward / coarsening-upward packages bounded by regional shales.

The disadvantage: the letter scheme is opaque to outsiders. There are no formation names like "Crocker" or "Gomantong" to anchor an outsider's mental model. This is one reason the Malay Basin reads as a mature, less-frontier province to non-Malaysian eyes — the geology is well-understood but the naming convention is locally tied.

03Why there is oil here

A working petroleum system needs five elements lined up in space and time. The Malay Basin has them all. The proven play is the Middle Miocene Anticline Play (Group E/F reservoirs in anticlinal closures formed by Miocene inversion). Two emerging plays are still being tested.

SOURCE

Group L/M lacustrine + H/I/J coal

Dual source: Type I/II lacustrine shales (deep kitchens) + Type III/II-III coal and coaly shales (coastal-plain). TOC 1.5–5.0% in coaly shales. Generation onset: Middle Miocene for H/I/J, Early Miocene for L/M.

RESERVOIR

Groups D, E, F, H, I, J (clastic)

Deltaic to fluvial-deltaic sandstones. Porosity 12–28%, permeability 10–1000 mD. Best fields: Dulang (Group E), Angsi (Groups I/J), Sotong (Groups H/I). Multiple pay zones stacked in single closures.

SEAL

Intraformational shales + Group F regional

Group F marine shale is a continuous regional seal, 50–150 m thick. Intraformational shales throughout the section provide local seals. Seal-to-reservoir thickness ratio averages 1.5×.

TRAP

Fault-bounded anticlines (inversion)

Middle–Late Miocene compression reversed the older normal faults, pushing hanging walls up into anticlinal closures. Seal-dependence: high (SGR-controlled). Stratigraphic pinchouts provide secondary trap type in Group H/I.

MIGRATION

Vertical via faults, lateral via carriers

Vertical migration along fault conduits. Lateral migration along deltaic carrier beds from deep kitchens to shallow anticlinal traps. Timing: Miocene to Present (active today).

KEY EMERGING PLAY

Western Hinge Fault zone (2024–2025)

LEBAH EMAS-1 (2025) opened a new play fairway along the western hinge fault zone. The hinge zone had been considered high-risk; the wildcat proved the play works. PSC awards via MBR+ (Malaysia Bid Round Plus) Round I continue to focus here.

Three plays at three risk levels: the Middle Miocene Anticline Play is proven and highly successful. The Early Miocene Stratigraphic Pinchout Play (Group H/I fluvial sands, channel pinchouts) is emerging with high stratigraphic risk. The Deep Syn-rift Play (Group K/L sandstones, fault-block closures) is high-risk, underexplored — but the 2025 BEKANTAN-1 and PUTERI BASEMENT-1 campaigns are beginning to de-risk it.

How to read this section

Read the section like a tape recorder. The left edge is onshore Peninsular Malaysia (granite basement of the Main Range). The Western Hinge Fault is the structural divide — the place where the basin drops off and the founder's "new play" LEBAH EMAS-1 was drilled in 2025. The central depocentre has the thickest section (~10–12 km). The right edge is the eastern flank, where BEKANTAN-1 (2018) found the shallowest flowing oil and PUTERI BASEMENT-1 (2020) found hydrocarbons in fractured granite beneath the basin fill.

Look for the anticlinal closures — the Middle Miocene Anticline Play. These are the proven traps. They sit in Groups D, E, F, H, I. The deeper the closure, the more chance of stratigraphic complications (the Emerging Play).

05Founder wells (4) — plays the founder signed off

Four PETRONAS Carigali wildcats the founder put his name on. Each one opened something — a play, a fairway, a reservoir concept, or a frontier. Together they sketch the recent evolution of the basin.

SHALLOWEST OIL · 2018

BEKANTAN-1

MALAY BASIN · PETRONAS · 2018

Shallowest flowing oil in the Malay Basin. The well proved that the upper section of the basin (Groups D–F) can deliver producible oil at shallower depths than the standard play. Important for the Small Field Asset PSC economics.

FRACTURED BASEMENT · 2020

PUTERI BASEMENT-1

MALAY BASIN · PETRONAS · 2020

Fractured basement test — hydrocarbons in fractured granite beneath the main basin fill. This is the analog of the Cuu Long Basin (Vietnam) basement play, applied for the first time to the Malay Basin. The Deep Syn-rift Play needs deep kitchens and a working basement carrier; this well tested both.

NEW PLAY FAIRWAY · 2025

LEBAH EMAS-1

MALAY BASIN · PETRONAS · 2025

New play wildcat that opened a new play fairway along the western hinge fault zone. The hinge zone had been high-risk; the wildcat proved the play works. PSC awards via MBR+ (Malaysia Bid Round Plus) Round I in 2024 already anticipated this — and now have a working concept to chase.

SMALL FIELD ASSET · 2024

BUNGA TASBIH-1

MALAY BASIN · PETRONAS · 2024

Small Field Asset PSC award — Malaysia Bid Round Plus (MBR+) Round I. Structural / stratigraphic trap, in the standard Middle Miocene play. The pattern: MBR+ awards small discovered fields to operators who can move them to production fast, lowering the cost of capital for the basin's mature fields.

Reading the four together: 2018 found shallow oil (BEKANTAN-1), 2020 found basement oil (PUTERI BASEMENT-1), 2024 was awarded a small field (BUNGA TASBIH-1), 2025 opened a new hinge fairway (LEBAH EMAS-1). The pattern: mature basin, four different ways to grow. The founder was on each of these sign-off sheets.

06What we don't know

The Malay Basin is mature, but not closed. Four questions are open as of mid-2026. None are blocking, but all of them shape where the next wildcat goes.

○ OPEN QUESTIONS · MALAY BASIN · 2026-07

  • Fault seal risk (SGR) is highly sensitive to clay-content estimates in shale gouges. The fault-bounded anticline trap depends on fault-sealing capacity; one bad gouge estimate and the closure leaks. Mitigation: high-resolution GR logs + petrophysical uncertainty ensembles.
  • Deep reservoir quality degrades with depth — compaction and diagenesis reduce permeability in Group K/L below 3,000 m. The deep syn-rift play is real but the reservoir quality is the limiting factor. Mitigation: Faust/Gardner parameter checks calibrated vs core.
  • Western Hinge Fault zone was just opened by LEBAH EMAS-1 in 2025. The fairway is new; the play concept is new; PSC blocks are being awarded in real time. The next 12–18 months will determine if this becomes the basin's third major play or stays a one-off.
  • Source rock distribution at the deep basin axis is poorly imaged. The Group L/M lacustrine shales are the master source, but their lateral continuity and total organic carbon at depths >5 km is uncertain. Seismic + deep wells are the only path to resolution.
