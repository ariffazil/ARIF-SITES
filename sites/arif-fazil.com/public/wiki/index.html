Theory of Anomalous Contrast (ToAC)

Visual contrast ≠ physical reality. Every pixel transform chain must be audited before an interpretation can receive a SEAL.

Core Principle

The ToAC states that anomalies visible in seismic or geospatial imagery are contrast artefacts until proven otherwise. A bright spot on a seismic section may be a genuine DHI (Direct Hydrocarbon Indicator) or it may be a processing artefact, multiple, or geometric effect.

GEOX enforces an audit of the full transform chain — from acquisition to display — before allowing any geological interpretation to proceed. This is the implementation of Floor F2 (Truth: τ ≥ 0.99) and Floor F4 (Clarity: CRS + units mandatory).

Audit Requirements

Before GEOX renders a SEAL verdict on any interpretation, the following must be documented:

  • Coordinate reference system (CRS) and datum
  • Acquisition geometry and fold
  • Processing sequence (demultiple, migration, filter)
  • Display parameters (gain, colour scale, clip level)
  • Well tie quality (if available)

Baseline + Excess Pattern (Eureka E3 — wired 2026-06-03)

Every anomaly result must now carry a toac pair: baseline (expected signal if no anomaly) plus excess (delta beyond baseline + uncertainty). This is ToAC at the envelope level — not just inside ac_risk.py. The pair travels with every result so downstream consumers can audit "why was this flagged?" without re-running the physics.

What is Geological Analogy?

Analogical thinking in geology is Relational Structure Mapping, not surface resemblance. A geologist does not say "this rock looks like that rock." They say: 

"This PATTERN here behaves like that PATTERN there —
but under different pressure, scale, time, fluid, stress, burial, or erosion."

Same causal mechanics. Different physical expression. This is why expert geologists can interpret a basin they have never seen by mapping its structural grammar onto one they know.

Why It Is Rare

Genuine geological analogy requires simultaneous cognitive coupling across three normally incompatible dimensions:

DimensionHuman defaultGeologist requirement
TimeSeconds–yearsMillions–billions of years (Deep Time)
ScaleHuman-body scaleAtom → tectonic plate (12+ orders of magnitude)
EvidenceDirect observationSparse proxies, indirect signals, incomplete rock record

Most people pattern-match on surface similarity (colour, texture, shape). Expert analogical reasoning maps causal mechanisms — the forces, fluids, temperatures, and time budgets that produced the pattern. This de-coupling from human-scale time and re-coupling to the Earth's metabolic pulse is what makes the skill exceptionally rare.

Theory · Mathematics

Entropy Constraint (ΔS ≤ 0)

Floor F4 — every action in the system must reduce or maintain information entropy. Actions that increase confusion are constitutionally blocked.

Definition

In information-theoretic terms, entropy S is the measure of uncertainty or disorder in a state. The constitutional constraint ΔS ≤ 0 means that every action an agent takes must leave the system in a state of equal or lower uncertainty than before.

Concretely: if an agent's response makes the situation less clear — adds ambiguity, contradicts established facts, or leaves a problem in a worse-understood state — it violates F4.

Practical enforcement

  • Every tool call must declare how it reduces uncertainty (even if marginally)
  • Responses that introduce more unknowns than they resolve score negative ΔS and are blocked
  • In GEOX, this manifests as the requirement for coordinates + CRS on all inputs — bare pixel values have undefined entropy reduction
  • In AF-FORGE, multi-step plans must show monotonically decreasing uncertainty toward the goal
