3. PETROS — The Existential Gas Variable

The single largest non-price risk to PETRONAS's gas business is the constitutional dispute with Petroleum Sarawak Berhad (PETROS). Sarawak holds approximately 60% of Malaysia's natural gas reserves and accounts for 90% of the nation's LNG exports. PETROS was established under Sarawak's Oil Mining Ordinance 1958, and the state government asserts regulatory authority over oil and gas within its territorial boundaries.

4. Strait of Hormuz — The LNG Supply Shock

The closure of the Strait of Hormuz since 28 February 2026 has removed approximately 20% of global LNG supply (Qatar + UAE). PETRONAS, as a non-Gulf LNG exporter, benefits from the resulting price spike — its Bintulu LNG complex is not transiting the Strait.

6. Gas Infrastructure — PETGAS & PGU

PETRONAS Gas Berhad (PETGAS, 6033.KL) is the listed gas infrastructure arm. It operates the Peninsular Gas Utilisation (PGU) pipeline network, gas processing plants, and regasification terminals. PETGAS is the most stable earnings contributor in the PETRONAS group — fee-based, quasi-regulated, with flat dividends.
